What this actually computes

A helical compression spring is linear over its working range, so everything follows from the rate:

F = k · x    x = L₀ − L    k = G d⁴ / (8 D³ N⍐)

d = wire diameter, D = mean diameter = OD − d, N⍐ = active coils, G = shear modulus.

Why the coil count matters even though vendors do not print it

McMaster lists OD, ID, wire diameter, free length and rate — but not coil count, and therefore not solid height. Without solid height you cannot tell whether your working point is even reachable. So the rate equation is inverted for N⍐, the end treatment gives total coils and solid height from there, and travel to solid falls out. Those derived numbers are marked derived everywhere they appear.

N⍐ = G d⁴ / (8 D³ k)    Nₜ = N⍐ + inactive coils    Lₛ = Nₜ d (ground ends)

Usable travel

Vendor max deflection is used when published. Otherwise usable travel is taken as 85% of travel to solid: running a stock spring down to solid is how it takes a permanent set and stops being the spring you specified.

Stress

τ = Kₛ · 8 F D / (π d³)    Kₛ = 1 + 0.5/C    C = D/d

Allowable is a fraction of ultimate tensile strength, itself a function of wire diameter (Sₜ = A/dṁ, Shigley table 10-4): 45% for music wire, 35% for austenitic stainless. The Wahl factor is reported alongside for anyone taking the spring into cyclic duty, where it is the one to use.

The number most calculators skip: how tightly can you hold the force?

Force error comes from two places — where the spring actually ends up (dF/dx = k, so a stiff spring punishes assembly slop) and the rate tolerance of the spring itself (±10% is normal for stock, commercial-grade springs). Both are reported, worst case and RSS. This is why a softer spring compressed further beats a stiff one nudged slightly, every time.

Buckling

Small-diameter springs get slender fast. The Shigley criterion is applied: below a critical free length the spring is stable at any deflection; above it, a critical deflection is reported, and how the ends are guided changes it by a factor of four.

Assumptions and limits

  • Round wire, helical compression springs, static or low-cycle duty. No fatigue life, no surge frequency.
  • Rate is treated as constant. Real springs stiffen in the last 15–20% of travel as the end coils close.
  • Shear modulus is a single published value per material; music wire actually varies a few percent with wire size.
  • Nothing here is a substitute for measuring the spring you were actually shipped.

Where the numbers come from

No spring data is bundled. Catalogue rows are whatever you paste in or import, and every row keeps the part number and a link back to the vendor page so you can check it. The example catalogue is synthetic — geometrically consistent, useful for trying the tool, and not real vendor parts. Do not order from it.
